**What I'm trying to understand**

How the body’s many parts work together, and what changes when things go wrong. We collect extraordinary amounts of information about people. I’m curious about how much more we could understand if we could connect those pieces.

**What I'm currently building**

A lab where people from different backgrounds can follow a good question somewhere unexpected. My own path has taken a few turns, so I have a soft spot for that. How to build the lab I just described. I know how I’d like people to feel here; I’m still learning what I need to do to make that happen. When to help, when to step back, and how to notice when someone needs something they haven’t asked for.

**What I would love to collaborate on**

I’d love to work with clinicians on the things that make them say, “I wish we could…” I like building things, and I get excited about what new imaging and computing technologies might let us do. I’d want to sit down with you, understand what would actually help, and see what we can make work. I won’t necessarily know how at the start. That’s part of what makes it interesting.

## Benedict Schildberg, approved 29 October 2026

**Display name (PI instruction, 29 October 2026):** Benedict Schildberg, M.Sc. Omit Peter from the team profile; keep publication author records unchanged.

The PI approved the following four answers for publication, focusing on LUMOS, temporal graphs and what the sequence of clinical events can add to understanding disease. The PI explicitly removed the LION strand from this profile. Preserve the remaining wording verbatim.

**What I'm trying to understand**

How much more can we learn about disease when we connect what happened to a patient, when it happened, and what followed? I’m interested in what AI could help us understand when we preserve that history.

**What I'm currently building**

I’m building LUMOS to turn scattered clinical records into connected patient histories. We represent these as temporal graphs, linking clinical events and keeping track of how they unfold over time. I want to explore whether those connections help us understand disease better.

**What I haven't figured out yet**

When is extracting the facts enough, and when do we need to reason about how they connect over time? I’m still working out how to tell whether a more complete history gives us a more useful understanding.

**What I would love to collaborate on**

I’d like to work with clinicians and researchers on questions where the patient’s history matters. What would you want to understand if you could follow the whole story across reports, scans and years of care? Those are the questions I’d like to bring to our work on temporal graphs and AI.

## Maissa Monsour, approved 29 October 2026

The following four answers were supplied and approved for publication. Preserve the wording verbatim.

**What I'm trying to understand**

What becomes possible when AI learns medicine the way clinicians do, by combining every source of information, and uses that knowledge to look ahead.

**What I'm currently building**

I’m building multimodal AI that brings different types of medical data together into one coherent picture of each patient, so that we can move from describing disease to predicting how it will develop. Alongside this, I’m developing autonomous AI agents that read medical images, apply established clinical guidelines and turn their findings into structured assessments. These agents are designed to adapt as medical knowledge and guidelines evolve, so that their results stay consistent, transparent and trustworthy over time.

**What I haven't figured out yet**

How do we know when an AI system is truly ready to be trusted in clinical practice?

**What I would love to collaborate on**

Projects that open the black box of medical AI, where every prediction comes with reasoning a clinician can understand, question and trust.
